Abstract

We present a vision-guided pick-and-place system for a UR3e arm mounted on a linear rail, simulated in MuJoCo. This is our own project, close in scope to Track B. A wrist-mounted RGB-D camera, calibrated from checkerboard views, measures the pixel position and depth of a randomly placed can. A Kalman filter fuses repeated measurements into a 3D position estimate with quantified uncertainty. Joint torques come from per-joint LQR controllers driven by observer estimates of angle and speed, with gravity feed-forward, while the rail has its own Kalman filter and discrete LQR. Position-based visual servoing (PBVS) brings the gripper above the can before it is grasped and placed on a target plate. We evaluate [N] randomised trials and report estimation consistency, tracking error, control effort and placement accuracy.

1. Modelling

Joints. Each of the six arm joints is modelled as a rotating inertia with state x = [θ, θ̇] and torque input τ. With friction set to zero, the model is a double integrator: θ̈ = (k/J)τ, so A = [[0, 1], [0, 0]], B = [0; k/J], C = [1, 0]. The inertia J of each joint is read from the UR3e mass matrix in MuJoCo (diagonal entries at the zero pose), and k = 1.

Controllability and observability. Both matrices have rank 2 for every joint, so the full state can be driven by torque and reconstructed from the angle measurement alone. Speed is not measured directly. It is observable because it is the rate of change of the measured angle.

Rail. The base slides on a prismatic joint with damping 100 and force limit ±200 N. The model is a discrete position and velocity system (stepped at 2 ms) with position measured by an encoder.

Assumptions. Joints are treated as independent (coupling between joints is ignored), inertia is constant (taken at one pose), and gravity is handled by feed-forward rather than in the model. Remaining coupling acts as a disturbance that feedback must reject.

2. Frames

The chain from sensor to the quantity the controller acts on:

  1. Pixel (u, v) and depth Z from the wrist camera.
  2. Camera frame, computer-vision convention: PC = Z · K-1[u, v, 1]T.
  3. Axis flip by [1, −1, −1] to the MuJoCo camera convention (camera looks along −z).
  4. World frame: PW = pcam + Rcam PC.
  5. Subtract the can height (0.135 m) to get the can base, which is the PBVS target.

The rail position shifts the robot base within the world frame, so the rail estimate affects the whole chain.

4. Estimation

Joint observer. A Luenberger-type observer runs a copy of the model and corrects it with the encoder: x̂̇ = A x̂ + B u + L (y − C x̂). The gain L is the steady-state Kalman gain from lqe with process noise Q = diag(1e-3, 1) and measurement noise R = 1e-5. Larger Q/R makes the estimate faster and noisier. Smaller Q/R makes it smoother and slower. [State how you checked or tuned these values, e.g. the sweep in the results.]

Rail Kalman filter. Estimates rail position and velocity from the noisy encoder.

Can Kalman filter. The can is stationary, so the state is its 3D position with A = I, C = I. Each photo is one noisy measurement. The filter starts at the centre of the spawn area with 0.2 m standard deviation, uses a small process noise of 0.002 m, and builds R from the pixel noise converted to metres (σxy = 1 px × Z/f), the depth noise at 0.45 m, and the 3 mm bias. Consistency is checked with NEES: for a 3-state filter the mean should be near 3, and about 95% of trials should fall below 7.815.

5. Control

Joint LQR. Each joint uses u = −K(x̂ − xref) with weights Q = diag(100, 1) and R = 1, which penalises angle error 100 times more than speed error. Gains are computed per joint from its inertia. The controller uses the observer's estimate, not the true state. [Paste the printed LQR gains here.]

Gravity feed-forward. Gravity torque at the estimated pose is added to each command, so LQR only corrects the remaining error.

Rail. A discrete LQR with weights diag(1000, 1) and R = 0.01 acts on the Kalman estimate, with force clipped at ±200 N.

PBVS. The error is the gripper position error to a point 15 cm above the estimated can, plus a tilt error that keeps the gripper pointing down. Joint velocities are q̇ = J+ λ e with λ = 2, applied for 0.2 s per step, repeating with a fresh photo each step until the error is below 2 mm. Limits: motor torque limits, noise passing through the observer, and ignored joint coupling.

Limitations.

  • Joint coupling is ignored and inertia is held constant at one pose.
  • Noise values are injected by us, so robustness is to our noise models rather than real sensor behaviour.
  • Some simulator poses (camera pose, gripper pose and Jacobian) are used by the visual-servo kinematics instead of values computed from the estimates. [Confirm or change this once you have decided how to handle it.]
  • If the can leaves the image, no measurement is available and the code does not yet skip the update.
